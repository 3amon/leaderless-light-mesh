Normal mode, one ring on its own. Both panels are real firmware output: the shipped v0.1.90 renderer on the left, the working tree on the right, each run through main.c's vivid guard, overflow rescale, 45% brightness and the slewed 45/LED power cap. 16 LEDs, 20 fps, 16 s loop starting after the minute crossfade. Palette picks three different minutes of the same family: the anchor hue walks 47° per minute on both sides, and on the new side the companion spread (22, 34, 48 or 64°) and its side are re-rolled each minute too.

Load / led is the mean summed-channel value per pixel after brightness, the number the rail actually sees; the firmware cap is 45. Capped frames is how often the limiter had to dim the frame. Blended sat is the whole ring averaged together, what a heavy cap does physically: 1.00 vivid, 0.00 white. Exposure only scales the drawing: true is the raw 8-bit value the strip receives, which a monitor renders far dimmer than an LED. Every family keeps a single anchor hue and only leans toward an analogous companion, never a complement, so the blend stays saturated.
